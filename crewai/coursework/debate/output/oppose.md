I oppose the motion.

AI should **not** be run in a tightly controlled way because over-control slows innovation, concentrates power, and can prevent society from benefiting from one of the most transformative technologies in history.

First, **heavy control stifles progress**. AI develops quickly, and strict rules often lag behind reality. If we force AI into rigid systems of approval and restriction, smaller innovators, researchers, and startups will be blocked, while only large corporations and governments can afford compliance. That does not create safety — it creates a monopoly.

Second, **control can become a tool of censorship and power**. If AI is centrally controlled, the people deciding the rules also decide what information, tools, and capabilities others are allowed to access. That opens the door to bias, political abuse, and unfair limitation of knowledge. A technology as important as AI should be open enough to serve everyone, not restricted by a few gatekeepers.

Third, **responsible use is better than excessive control**. The answer to risk is not to slow AI to a crawl, but to build better standards, transparency, and accountability. We should focus on education, clear ethics, and targeted safeguards against real harm — not blanket control that punishes useful innovation before it even begins.

In short, AI should be **guided**, not tightly controlled. Excessive control protects institutions more than people, and it risks turning a powerful tool for human progress into a restricted privilege for only a few.
